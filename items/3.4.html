3.4 Adversarial analysis: what prevents miner-validator collusion on false attestation

Tier 1 Filed, partial

In preparation. Written collusion analysis with stake sizes, honeypot rates and the economic bound on a coordinated false attestation.

A miner and a validator who agree to certify work that was never done face five documented defences: evidence signed in hardware at capture, dual stakes in KNX and stablecoins that are slashed on proven collusion, honeypot reference tasks with a hidden audit set that catch validators who approve failed runs, consensus-alignment penalties that cut the standing of a validator whose scores drift from the set, and deterministic simulation before settlement. The design intent is that the expected cost of a false attestation exceeds its upside. The bound itself is not yet quantified: stake sizes, honeypot rates and slashing tiers are governance parameters without published values.

The attack

The record that settles a job binds a signed task instruction, a policy execution trace, a sensor bundle and an independent validator score. A colluding pair needs either a fabricated sensor bundle that passes verification, or a validator willing to score a failed or absent run as passed. The first is a provenance attack on the evidence, the second a corruption attack on scoring. The defences below address them in that order.

How the defences combine

For a coordinated false attestation to pay, the pair must defeat provenance and scoring at once. With hardware-signed evidence the miner cannot forge the bundle without the device key; with honeypots the validator cannot tell whether the job it is asked to approve is audited; with consensus alignment a single corrupt validator is outvoted and penalised, so the coalition must control enough stake-weighted validators on the subnet to move the aggregate; with dual stake the coalition's KNX and stablecoin tranches are at risk on discovery. The whitepaper's settlement rule closes the loop: on a failed PoPW the executor's stablecoin deposit is slashed and the payer refunded, and a validator that wrongly approves compensates the harmed party from its assurance stake.

What is not yet quantified

The economic bound depends on parameters that are set by governance per subnet phase and have not been published: the coefficients α, β and γ of the metascore, the honeypot injection rate and the spot-check rate, the slashing fractions per severity tier, the minimum validator stake per subnet and the number of validators whose agreement moves the aggregate. Until those are fixed, the analysis above is qualitative. On the testnet the three live subnets have 3, 3 and 2 registered validators respectively, and each subnet's latest weight publication assigns full weight to a single miner UID, so the consensus-alignment term has not yet operated across a wide validator set.
