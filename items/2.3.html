2.3 Emissions design memo: schedule, and how issuance responds to demand

Tier 1 TOKEN Filed

Emissions are paid from a pre-minted pool of 430,000,000 KNX (43.00% of supply), not from inflation: total supply never exceeds 1,000,000,000 KNX. Release starts at T+4 under a hard annual cap of 48,000,000 KNX in the first year that steps down 15% each year, and actual issuance is the lesser of that cap and a usage-linked formula paying KNX per verified attested task, with the per-task rate halving annually. What is not issued under the cap stays in the pool as a ceiling; it is not scheduled supply.

This bound is arithmetic on the stated parameters.

How issuance responds to demand

Issuance is bounded above by the cap and is driven below the cap by verified work: the usage-linked formula pays KNX per verified attested task, and the per-task rate halves each year. A network verifying few tasks issues less than the cap; a network verifying many tasks issues the cap and no more. The parameters of the formula (the initial KNX per task, the definition of a verified attested task and the halving date) are not yet published.

Demand for KNX is created on the other side of the ledger by functions that require KNX to be held or spent while task settlement stays in stablecoins. Each is a design statement from the whitepaper or the; the pre-TGE ERC-20 carries none of these functions, which activate at mainnet or in later protocol phases.

The Monetary Policy tab declares the token as fixed supply with decreasing issuance and states that protocol revenue is directed to open-market buy-and-burn from TGE. The fee that feeds the buyback is the 50 bps (0.50%) stable fee on executor payouts.

Why a declining, usage-linked cap

the schedule replaces that with a cap that falls on a clock and an issuance rule that only reaches the cap when verified work supports it, so the supply released to miners and validators tracks the work the network actually attests rather than the calendar alone.
