2.6 Token classification legal opinion: US plus issuing jurisdiction

Tier 2 TOKEN Filed, partial

In preparation. Opinions under US law and under the law of the issuing jurisdiction (BVI) and of the Foundation (Panama). The two opinions on file cover Singapore and Korea only.

Two token-classification opinions are filed. Chung Ting Fai & Co (Singapore, 3 February 2026) concludes that KNX is unlikely to be a security token or other capital-markets product under the Singapore Securities and Futures Act, and unlikely to be a digital payment token or e-money under the Payment Services Act. Lee & Ko (Seoul, 24 July 2026) concludes that KNX is unlikely to be a security under the Korean Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act. Neither opinion addresses US law, the law of the British Virgin Islands or the law of Panama.

Singapore: Chung Ting Fai & Co, 3 February 2026

PointContent of the opinion
AddresseeKonnex Inc (Registration No. C-129951), Liberia, described as the developer of the KNX Token. Reference CTF/INC/2602/9578.
QuestionWhether KNX is a capital markets product (security token) under the laws of Singapore.
BasisThe KNX Whitepaper dated 26 July 2025 (Annex A to the opinion) and publicly available material at the date of the opinion. The token uses relied on: rewards to validators through staking or locking; a deposit that can be slashed for wrong or incomplete validations; settlement of platform fees; participation in protocol governance. Given on the understanding that the company has no operations or customers in Singapore and does not intend to offer KNX to customers in Singapore.
AnalysisKNX is not akin to a share (no legal or beneficial interest in the company or any entity; governance relates to platform features, not the company), not a debenture (no debt acknowledged), not a derivative (tracks no underlying thing) and not a unit in a collective investment scheme (no managed property, no pooling of contributions or profits). Under the Payment Services Act, KNX is expressed as a unit and transferable electronically but is intended to settle fees internally within the platform and is unlikely to be a medium of exchange accepted by the public or a section of the public, so unlikely to be a digital payment token; it is not e-money.
ConclusionKNX is unlikely a security token or any other token which references regulated capital markets products under the Securities and Futures Act in Singapore.
Scope limits stated in the opinionSingapore law only, as at 3 February 2026; no view on the laws of any other country. Strictly limited to the KNX token itself according to its stated characteristics; no review of and no view on the Konnex platform, its features, services or any later development. No duty to update for changes in facts or law. Based on published MAS guidelines; no guarantee that MAS or a court would reach the same view.

Korea: Lee & Ko, 24 July 2026

PointContent of the opinion
AddresseeDalithium Inc, attention of the Chief Business Development Officer. Reference O530CG; 14 pages including cover; Korean language.
QuestionWhether KNX is a security under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act (FSCMA), in response to the query of 9 July 2026.
BasisThe whitepaper provided on 9 July 2026 and the company's e-mail of 16 July 2026. Token functions analysed: payment of protocol and network fees; staking collateral for validators, executors and AI miners with slashing for misconduct (a delegation mechanism noted as under consideration); governance over protocol parameters such as the dual-staking parameters and model-registry flags; rewards and incentives for network participants; and the buyback mechanism by which a fee on stablecoin settlement flows is swapped to KNX for the protocol treasury.
AnalysisAssessed against the general definition of a security and each of the six statutory sub-types, with particular attention to the investment-contract security, applying the Financial Services Commission's 2023 token-security guidelines. The functions attached to KNX (means of payment, service assurance, governance participation, rewards and platform benefits) are unlikely to constitute a contractual right to the profits or losses of a common enterprise, and the issuer makes no promise to distribute business profits to holders; the elements of the investment-contract test are therefore unlikely to be met.
ConclusionThe likelihood that KNX meets the general definition of a security under the FSCMA is low, and the likelihood that it falls within any of the sub-types is also low; KNX is therefore unlikely to be a security under the FSCMA.
Scope limits stated in the opinionBased solely on the description provided; the regulator assesses securities status case by case on all circumstances, including implicit agreements, smart-contract execution, profit distribution and promotional statements, so a different or changed description could change the conclusion. Based on regulatory interpretations and court decisions published to date, which may change. Refers to the United States SEC interpretive guidance of 27 March 2026 and to Korean case law as context only; gives no opinion on foreign law.

What the two opinions do not cover

  • United States federal or state securities law, including any analysis of the private rounds, the equity investment or the current round.
  • The law of the British Virgin Islands (jurisdiction of the token issuer, Dalithium Inc) and the law of Panama (jurisdiction of Dalithium Foundation).
  • The European Union, including any classification under MiCA (the company's position that KNX is not an asset-referenced or e-money token is stated in item 7.2, not in an opinion).
  • The Konnex platform, its settlement and escrow mechanics, staking rewards, the buyback flywheel or any activity of the company; both opinions address the token as described, not the business.
  • The revised whitepaper of June 2026 in the case of the Singapore opinion, which rests on the 26 July 2025 whitepaper; the Korean opinion rests on the whitepaper supplied on 9 July 2026 and the 16 July 2026 e-mail.
  • Any exchange-listing, custody, market-making or distribution activity, and any question of licensing of the company itself.

The Singapore opinion is addressed to Konnex Inc, the company's earliest entity; the Korean opinion is addressed to Dalithium Inc, the token issuer. The relationship between the two entities is set out in item 1.2.
