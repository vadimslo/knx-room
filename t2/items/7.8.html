7.8 Patents, freedom-to-operate analysis and open-source licence inventory

Tier 2 Filed, partial

In preparation. Patent position and freedom-to-operate analysis; the licence inventory of the public repositories is in the room.

The public Konnex code is released under permissive terms: the chain runtime under the Unlicense and the SDK, the drone subnet and the robot gateway under MIT. All four repositories are forks or derivatives of third-party open-source projects (Bittensor subtensor and SDK, the Bittensor subnet template, OpenFly-Platform, OpenClaw), whose obligations are limited to retaining copyright and licence notices, with one point to manage: the upstream subtensor repository moved from the Unlicense to Apache-2.0 in July 2026, so any upstream code merged after that date carries Apache-2.0 notice and patent terms.

Effect on Konnex's own code

None of the upstream licences is copyleft at the repository level, so Konnex is free to keep its additions private (the GitLab development model) and to license public releases as it chooses, provided the upstream notices remain. The Unlicense on the runtime fork means the published runtime carries no copyright claim by Konnex; anyone may copy it without attribution. The kitchen and SLAM subnet code, the explorer, the control plane and the token contract are not public and their licences are internal.
